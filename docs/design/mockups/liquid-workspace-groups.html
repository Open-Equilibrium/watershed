Liquid Workspace: Groups in Arrange mode

Mockups for review, asked for by the maintainer on 2026-11-01 after he approved the Pages mockups: several Blocks can form a group that moves and resizes as one tile, so a Page's text need not be arranged Block by Block, while each Block inside stays movable and resizable. The selection model follows Keynote, PowerPoint and Figma, the only apps of this kind with groups; Notion and AppFlowy offer columns only.

1 · Select several Blocks and group them

⇧-click or a drag across empty grid selects several tiles; the Arrange bar then offers Group (⌘G). Here the three Text Blocks of "Trip to Lisbon" are selected, while the photo and the route stay single.

2 · The group is one tile

The three Blocks now sit in one group at the place of the first, in their Page order. A click selects the whole group: it moves by its top handle and resizes by its right edge like any tile, and its menu renames or ungroups it. The Blocks inside follow.

3 · Arranging inside the group

A double-click or ⏎ enters the group: everything outside dims, the Arrange bar shows where you are, and the group's own columns appear, as many as it spans. Blocks inside move and resize in those columns, so two can sit side by side. esc leaves the group, a second esc leaves Arrange mode.

4 · Moving a Block into or out of a group

Dragging a tile over a group shows an insertion line and names the group; dropping it there moves the Block in. Dragging a Block past the group's edge moves it out onto the Page grid. Keyboard: ⌘G with a Block and a group selected adds it, ⇧⌘G ungroups.

5 · Narrow window and phone

A group counts as one tile for the narrow-window rule, and the Blocks inside follow the same rule within it. On a phone every Block stacks in Page order; the grouped Blocks are consecutive, so they stay together.

6 · Outside Arrange mode

Groups only show in Arrange mode; the Page reads as content. Typing at the end of a grouped Text Block, /, paste or drop below a grouped Block put the new Block into the same group, so writing never breaks the layout.

Assumptions for review

  1. Layout only. A group is part of the Page's layout: it holds Views, not Blocks, and has no rights, Connections or Automations of its own. The same Block on another Page is not affected. Grouping, moving and ungrouping are History Actions like any layout change.
  2. One level. No group inside a group, so selection, keyboard and phone order stay predictable.
  3. Order. Grouping makes the Blocks consecutive in Page order at the place of the first one, keeping their order. Ungrouping leaves them in place, each as wide as the group was.
  4. Size. A group spans whole Page columns; inside it has as many columns as it spans. Its height follows its content, so growing text pushes what is below and never overlaps.
  5. Selection as in Keynote and Figma: a click selects the group, a double-click or ⏎ enters it, esc leaves; ⇧-click adds to the selection; ⌘G groups and ⇧⌘G ungroups; arrow keys move, ⌥ and the arrow keys resize.
  6. Lifetime. A group lives as long as it holds a Block; when the last one leaves or is deleted, the group goes too. Deleting a Block inside follows the last-View rule as anywhere else.
  7. Name. Optional, "Group" by default, shown only in Arrange mode and to screen readers.
  8. Spec change. The V-Spec says Arrange mode places Blocks "without a separate container". On approval it would say that Arrange mode may group Views into one layout tile that owns no content.
