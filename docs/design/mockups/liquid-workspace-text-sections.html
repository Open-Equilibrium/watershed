Liquid Workspace: Sections

Approved by the maintainer on 2026-11-01; they replace the dropped groups idea. A Text Block is one paragraph, heading, list item or to-do; ⏎ ends it and starts the next, as in Notion. Consecutive Text Blocks form a Section, which --- or a Block of another type ends. In Arrange mode a Section is one tile, so text is arranged as a whole, while every other Block is its own tile. Shell and wording follow the Pages mockups.

1 · How Sections form

Hovering over text shows the Section's outline and, faintly, its Text Blocks. Here the first Section holds five Text Blocks and ends at ---; the second ends at the photo; the text after the photo starts a third. Nobody creates a Section: it follows from the Page's order.

2 · Arrange mode: one tile per Section

Each Section is a tile, named by its first line, beside the photo and the route. The Text Blocks inside keep their order; they move in the flow with their handle, while Arrange mode moves and resizes the whole Section. The divider is not drawn in Arrange mode, where tile edges show the boundary.

3 · Arranging inside a Section

A double-click or ⏎ on a Section's tile enters it, as the dropped groups did: everything outside dims, the Arrange bar shows where you are, and the Section's own columns appear, as many as it spans. Its Text Blocks then move and resize in those columns, so text can run in two or three columns. esc leaves the Section. On a phone the Text Blocks stack in their Page order again.

4 · Splitting and joining Sections

--- and ⏎ between two Text Blocks splits their Section. ⌫ at the start of the Text Block below a divider removes the divider and joins both Sections. This changes only the layout, so it needs no dialog and Undo reverts it. Inserting a Block of another type between Text Blocks also splits a Section, and deleting it joins them again.

Decisions

Approved (2026-11-01)

  1. Text Block. One paragraph, heading, list item, to-do or quote; ⏎ ends it, as in Notion. A toggle stays formatting: the Text Blocks indented below it fold with it.
  2. Section is layout only. It holds no content and has no rights, Connections or Views of its own; it follows from the order of Text Blocks and their boundaries, like a column in Notion. Each Text Block stays a full Block with History, rights and Views; a View of one Text Block on another Page joins the Section it lands in there.
  3. Boundaries. A Section ends at ---, at any Block of another type and at the Page's start and end. --- right after a Block of another type stays plain text, since a boundary already exists. There is no decorative divider.
  4. Tiles. When two Sections join, the joined one keeps the upper Section's place and width; when one splits, the new lower Section starts below it at the same width.
  5. Phone and narrow windows. A Section is one tile for the half-or-full rule; on a phone everything stacks in Page order.

Approved later the same day

  1. Inside a Section as the dropped groups worked: enter with a double-click or ⏎, leave with esc; the Section's own columns, as many as it spans; its Text Blocks move and resize in them; the Page order stays the reading order on a phone and for screen readers.
  2. Markdown import follows CommonMark. Only a thematic break becomes a Section boundary: ---, *** or ___ on a line of its own. --- directly under a line of text underlines a heading (a setext heading, like ===), so it becomes a Text Block formatted as a heading and no boundary. A --- block at the very start is YAML front matter and becomes no boundary either; inside code and table rows --- stays text.
  3. Markdown export writes headings as # and ##, never underlined, and writes each Section boundary as --- with a blank line above, so a round trip never turns a boundary into a heading.
