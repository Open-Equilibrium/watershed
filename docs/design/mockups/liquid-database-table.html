Liquid Workspace: Table View

Mockups for the Table View of M3a's Database slice in PLAN.md, over the merged core and bridge: text, number, select, date and checkbox fields (ADR-0242, ADR-0243), rows read in windows (client architecture rule 3). The table's look follows the approved Blocks mockups, and the app's frame the app's structure (ADR-0241), at Notion's scale; frames here show only the Page. Approved by the maintainer on 2026-11-03, section 10 on 2026-11-06, with sort and filter deferred to the Views slice and long tables scrolling inside a fixed height. The Block is called Table in the app (ADR-0244, concept).

1 · A table on a Page

The Database Block shows its name, then a table: one column per field with the kind's icon, one row per row. Hovering a row shows its handle at the left; + New under the last row adds one, + after the last column adds a field. Numbers sit right-aligned and days read in the person's locale, options as colored tags. The footer counts the rows.

2 · Adding a table

Typing / in an empty Text Block opens slash commands, as in Notion and T3 Code: the Text Block styles that exist today and Table. Typing filters it, ↑↓ choose, ⏎ inserts. A new table has the text field "Name", no rows and its name field focused with the placeholder "Untitled".

3 · Adding a field

+ in the header opens a small panel: the field's name, then its kind. ↑↓ choose a kind; esc, a click outside, scrolling the table sideways or the Page, or resizing the window closes the panel. ⏎ or a click on a kind adds it after the last field; a field without a name takes its kind's, as in Notion. A field's kind cannot change yet; renaming happens in the field's menu (section 5).

4 · Editing cells

A click selects a cell, a second click or ⏎ edits it, and typing replaces it, as in a spreadsheet and in Notion. Text and numbers edit in place; a select cell and a date cell open a picker; a checkbox ticks on click or Space. A picker hangs under its cell, or above it when its options do not fit under it and there is more room above; its options scroll in the room left, ↑↓ wrapping around, and a cell out of view comes into view first. esc, a click outside, scrolling the table or the Page, resizing the window or the cell moving elsewhere closes it and keeps the value. In a date picker, weeks start as in the person's region (ADR-0246); ↑↓ choose the day after or before, from today when no day is typed, and ←→ move the caret. ⏎, ⇥ and a click outside write the day typed in the region's short form (11/1/2026), as cells show it (Nov 1, 2026) or as YYYY-MM-DD, with a year of four digits, and empty the cell when nothing is typed; anything else, two-digit years included, stays with “… is no date.”, which a click outside drops. A click on a day writes it and closes the picker; an empty cell opens on today's month. A number that is not one is refused in place and stays until it is fixed or undone. Leaving the editor keeps the edit, and an edit is written to the row it started on, also if that row moved meanwhile; if its row or field is gone, a number that is not one says “… It was not kept, as its row or field is gone.”, and “… as the Table closed.” when the Table closes.

5 · Field and row menus

Clicking a field's header opens its menu: rename it in place, or delete it. Deleting a field asks first, since its whole column goes. A row's handle opens its menu: insert a row above or below, or delete it without asking.

6 · Sort and filter, with the Views slice

The table's header holds Sort and Filter. The core sorts by one field and filters by one condition; each shows as a chip under the name, which opens it again or removes it. The footer says how many rows pass. They are built with the Views slice, which stores them on the View, so every device and person sees the same order, as in Notion.

7 · Long and wide tables

A table keeps a fixed height, as the Chat Block does: about ten rows, with its header and footer in place, and its rows scrolling inside; it reads them window by window as they scroll in. Arrange mode will change the height. A table shorter than that takes only its rows' height. Fields wider than the reading column scroll sideways inside the table, with the first field kept in view.

8 · Tag colors

Ten tag colors in tokens.css, as Notion's: default, gray, brown, orange, yellow, green, blue, purple, pink and red, each in light and dark. The core stores an option's color as a number; the number picks a color in this order, wrapping after ten. A new option takes the next color after the field's last option. Changing an option's name or color follows once the core can.

10 · Next to a table, and a Block Liquid does not know

A Block of a kind a later version adds shows as a quiet placeholder in its place (ADR-0245), never as text. The caret passes a table or a placeholder: ↑ or ← at a Text Block's start goes to the end of the Text Block above it, ↓ or → at the end to the start of the one below. ⌫ at the start of the Text Block below joins nothing, and a numbered list after it starts at 1 again.

Decisions

  1. The table, the field kinds' icons, + New, + for a field and the row count as in section 1; days and numbers in the person's locale; decimals, currencies and sums come with field formats.
  2. / opens slash commands with the Text Block styles and Table; the other Blocks follow with their slices.
  3. Cells select, then edit, as in section 4 and the keyboard table; a refused value stays in the cell with its reason.
  4. Deleting a field asks first; deleting a row does not. Both stay in the document, so History can restore them once it exists.
  5. Ten tag colors as tokens, a new option taking the next one.

Decided on 2026-11-03

  1. Sort and filter come with the Views slice, which stores them on the View; this slice builds the table without them.
  2. A long table keeps a fixed height and scrolls inside, like the Chat Block, rather than Notion's Load more.

Decided on 2026-11-06

  1. The caret passes a table or a placeholder, as section 10 shows; selecting a whole Block, as Notion does, follows with Block handles.
  2. Numbers and days read as in the person's region, Material's texts in their language, through intl and flutter_localizations (ADR-0246).
  3. What / opens is called slash commands, as in Notion and T3 Code; the Blocks mockups keep "Block menu" for a Block's own menu.
  4. A field without a name takes its kind's name, as in Notion; it stays English until localization is decided (ADR-0246).
  5. + New and + add last, which the core places as it applies the write (ADR-0247).

Decided on 2026-11-07

  1. esc on a selected cell puts the caret at the start of the Text Block below the table, if any; ⇥ goes on with the next row and stops at the table's last cell, as in Notion.
  2. A number that is not one stays in its editor until it is fixed or undone; one that cannot be kept says so, as section 4 shows.
  3. Liquid holds a table's cell writes not yet read back, so quick toggles flip; written values show when they are read back (ADR-0248).
  4. A date picker works as section 4 says: its ↑↓ step as macOS date fields do, it takes the three day forms with four-digit years and no shorter ones, ⇥ and a click outside keep the day typed as leaving an editor keeps the edit, and a click on a day writes and closes as a select picker's option does.
  5. Pickers and the field panel close when what they hang from moves (a scroll, a window resize, a change elsewhere), as Flutter's menus do, rather than locking the Page's scroll as Notion does; section 4 says where a picker opens.
